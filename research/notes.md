## The room

- **Walls:** deep bottle green (sampled #2D5034), crowded with framed Irish photos and postcards, a hand-drawn map of Ireland, a "Paddy" sign, a "More Blacks, More Dogs, More Irish" poster, books on shelves, an Egan's No. 8 Irish Whiskey mirror sign, a "…key Distilleries of Ireland" mural, hessian coffee sacks ("…Cork").
- **Wood and metal:** scrubbed timber tables and ledges, dark turned-leg bar stools with green leather tops, a black stove-style heater in a wooden fire surround, dark floorboards, exposed galvanised pipework used for shelving and the back-bar frame.
- **Back bar:** two tiers of bottles on lit shelves against black-and-white marble, a row of brass taps (Guinness, Murphy's, Camden, Mondo, Amstel tap badges), pewter tankards and copper jugs hanging from the pipework, a small fiddle and a tricolour tucked among the bottles, the Redbreast range on the lower shelf.
- **Light:** low and warm. Green enamel pendant shades, Edison bulbs, fairy lights through the ivy, tea lights in glass on every table. Photos are shot dark with warm highlights.
- **Ivy everywhere:** along the beams inside, over the shopfront outside.
- **Overhead:** GAA county pennants and hurling memorabilia hang from the ceiling (Brixton Buzz 2022 photos).
- **Snug:** listings call the whole interior "a cosy traditional Irish snug". It's small. Sessions happen round two pushed-together tables.
- **Shopfront:** red-painted pilasters, dark green frame, a "Céad Míle Fáilte" fascia, a dark brown hanging sign with mustard lettering and the tumbler mark, the tricolour on a pole, swan-neck lamps, ivy, hanging baskets of bright flowers, festoon bulbs.
- **Garden:** a small paved courtyard at the back ("private courtyard garden at the rear"). Open daily till 9pm (Brixton Buzz: "this closes early"). No photo of it exists anywhere I could reach.
- **Telly:** a screen in the green corner for GAA (their GAA advert shows GAA+ on it).

## How they talk

- Warm, plain, a bit cheeky. Short sentences, then a sign-off: "Sláinte!" (often with a shamrock emoji).
- From the site: "a traditional Irish cocktail bar with a modern twist", "a space where everyone feels at home", "just sit back, relax and raise a glass with us", "No reservations just walk in, grab a stool and enjoy!", "Good craic, great drinks and even better company", "A true taste of home", "a drop of the pure", "We have you covered for all the big GAA sporting events".
- From captions: "Same old creamy pints", "a cracking debut if we may say so ourselves!", "Low lights, great craic and some proper electric blues, shuffles and boogies", "we'd suggest a classic Manhattan or two", "did it ever really leave?", "It's a favourite for a reason and we love it so."
- From the menu: "Half pints are available but not encouraged", "Staff picks & bad decisions", "Winter Survival Menu", "It's a school night", "indecent amount of olives … utter filth", "Because regular coffee just wasn't enough."
- Spelling: "whiskey" throughout (keep it). They write "Seisiún", "Sláinte", "craic".
- Their typos on the live site: "Muprhy's", "along side", "Walk - ins", "whats on". The homepage says "Weiner"; the October menu says "Wiener". The site uses the menu spelling.

## Who comes when

- **After work, Mon–Fri 3–6pm:** happy hour (£8 cocktails, £5 Murphy's or Amstel, £5 wines). They open at 3pm on weekdays.
- **Weekend afternoons:** open from 1pm Sat–Sun, happy hour 1–4pm, the garden in daylight until 9pm.
- **Thursday nights:** Thursday Night Blues from 9pm, "Chicago style blues … played live in the heart of the bar". First night Thursday 24 September 2026 with John Drain; Marco Farris played on 8 October. Before that, Thursdays were acoustic sing-alongs with Molly's Chambers (2021–22).
- **Sundays:** Trad Seisiún from 6pm, "some of the London circuit's most talented musicians". It has run since at least 2022, with a summer break (The Session).
- **Match days:** the big GAA games on the screen. No 2026 fixtures published anywhere I could reach.
- **Late:** Fri and Sat till 1am, Tue–Thu and Sun till midnight, Mon till 11pm.
- **Who:** locals and Irish in London, groups of friends, dates; LGBTQ-friendly per one listing. Brixton Buzz: "one of Brixton Buzz's favourite watering holes".

## Reviewers

- **Google:** 4.6 from about 357 reviews (via Restaurant Guru, Sept 2026). TripAdvisor: 3.3 from 3 reviews.
- **Praised:** the staff. One Google review names Fin, Ruby and Ella for "hospitality and clear knowledge and passion for the drinks on offer". Also the atmosphere, the Guinness, the hot dogs, the whiskey range, the live music and the little garden. Time Out: "Brixton's only Irish cocktail bar". London x London: "lots of greens, lots of good music", drinks "served under domes of smoke".
- **Complaints:** being asked to move tables to fit a group in on a busy night (a long Google review and a TripAdvisor review). Prices "a step up". Security once called heavy-handed (secondhand, unverified). The courtyard closes early.
- **What this means for the site:** say clearly how walk-in-only works, before people arrive.

## What's weak on the current site

1. The "Location" heading has nothing under it. The address appears nowhere on their own site.
2. Both menus are PDFs: 8 MB of image-only pages for the house menu and 4 MB for the whiskey list. They can't be searched, are hard to read on a phone and are heavy on mobile data.
3. No phone number. An empty Squarespace cart ("0") sits in the header.
4. Hours are a static list. Nothing says whether they're open right now, whether happy hour is on, or what's on tonight.
5. The music section has no dates or names, though the Instagram captions on the same page do.
6. GAA: "check out whats on" goes nowhere.
7. Link preview: og:title is just "The Whiskey Tumbler", the description is empty, the image is the logo over http://, and twitter:card is "summary".
8. Typos ("Muprhy's", "Walk - ins", "whats"). Two homepage graphics are illustrations, not photos of the bar.

## Design reasoning

- **Palette:** from the logo, the room and their two menus. Night ink (#0E120F, the logo's black lifted towards the room's green shadow), wall green #2D5034, shamrock #399262 (sampled from the logo), chalk #EFEFEA (the logo's distressed white), shopfront oxblood #8A1F33, menu red #A80C0F (the October menu), parchment #E6D9C3 (whiskey list PDF), sign mustard #D7A940 (the hanging sign's lettering). The site's own tokens agree: accent green hsl(120 62% 25%), dark green hsl(148 72% 7%), cream hsl(42 100% 98%).
- **Type:** Cinzel (their site's heading font and the whiskey list's titles) for the whiskey shelf. Anton for the poster headings, closest to the condensed distressed caps on the October menu. Poppins (their site's body font) for reading text.
- **Layout:** two "papers" pinned in a dark green room. The house menu is set like their red-ruled poster menu, and the whiskey list is set like their parchment list with its double-rule frame and corner squares. Everything else sits on the green wall in low light, the way their photos are shot.
- **Phone first:** a bottom bar for thumbs. The first screen is a live board: open or closed and until when, happy hour, garden, tonight's music, and the address with directions.
- **Signature pieces:** (1) the tonight board on Europe/London time; (2) the back shelf, where bottles stand on shelves by country, you tap one to see it, and filters cover price, ABV and peated, with flights as a tray of three tumblers that fill; (3) the Wiener slip, tickable and resettable.
- **Motif:** their tumbler mark (from the logo and hanging sign) as the one recurring icon. The shamrock appears only inside their logo. No Celtic knots, no barrel wood, no gold script.

## What the owners most want this site to do

1. Get people walking in tonight: where it is, open now and until when, happy hour on now, what's on tonight, and how walk-ins work.
2. Make the 100+ whiskey list and the flights a reason to come, readable on a phone instead of a 4 MB PDF.
3. Fill the Thursday blues and the Sunday seisiún, with the GAA nights as a standing reason to drop in.
