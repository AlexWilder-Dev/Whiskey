   A brief history
 Irish whiskey dates back to at least the 12th century
  when Irish monks began distilling a spirit known as
            uisce beatha (ish-ka ba-ha),
              Gaelic for "water of life."


   Over the centuries it evolved into a refined and
  respected drink known for its smoothness thanks to
triple distillation, by the 19th century it was one of the
          most popular spirits in the world.


  However the 20th century brought major setbacks.
   Wars, trade disputes, Prohibition in the U.S. and
growing competition from Scotch whisky nearly wiped
 out the Irish industry. By the 1980s, only a handful of
                 distilleries remained.


 In recent years Irish whiskey has made a remarkable
  comeback. With new distilleries opening across the
country, renewed global interest and a focus on quality
 and tradition - Irish whiskey has become the fastest
growing spirit in the world, outpacing other categories
 in both sales and popularity and firmly reestablishing
         its place on the international stage.
